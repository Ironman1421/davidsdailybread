start with the restore test Trending workflowMakeUseOf front, Oct 3

The 3-2-1 backup rule leaves out the step that proves it: restore a file, and keep one copy ransomware cannot reach

A success message is not a backup; the only proof is a file you have actually pulled back out.

The familiar rule says keep three copies of your data on two kinds of media with one copy offsite, and MakeUseOf's Tashreef Shareef argues it stops one step short, because a backup is only as good as its restore. He cites a 2024 Backblaze survey in which 84 percent of computer owners said they back up their data while only 15 percent felt absolutely certain their important files were safe, and he extends the rule to 3-2-1-1-0: one of those copies immutable or air-gapped so ransomware cannot reach it, and zero errors confirmed by regular integrity checks. For the work itself he points to FreeFileSync for mirroring files to a drive you can disconnect afterwards and to Duplicati for the backup set, and he puts a real restore test on the calendar every quarter. The failures he is guarding against are quiet ones: files that corrupt in transfer, storage that degrades on the shelf, and incremental chains that break without telling anyone until the day you need them.

The tool shelf new tonight

FreeFileSync

Free; Donation Edition extraWindows, macOS, LinuxMakeUseOf, Oct 3

A folder comparison and synchronization tool that creates and manages backup copies, working out the differences between a source and a target and copying only what changed, with mirror, two-way and custom modes, batch jobs that can be scheduled, move detection so a renamed file is not copied again, Volume Shadow Copy support for files that are locked, binary comparison, paths longer than 260 characters, Google Drive, FTP, FTPS and SFTP targets, and a RealTimeSync companion, at version 14.12 from September 9, 2026; the limits are the license and the false comfort, since it is free for personal use while email notifications sit in the donor-only Donation Edition and commercial use needs a Business Edition license, and MakeUseOf's point stands that a sync run reporting success is still not evidence that the copy will restore.

draw.io

Free, Apache 2.0Browser, desktop, DockerXDA Developers, Oct 3

A diagramming editor that runs in a browser at app.diagrams.net, as a desktop download or from the project's own Docker image, with no sign-up and no paywall, covering flowcharts, network and UML diagrams, entity relationship charts, mind maps, org charts and floor plans from stocked shape libraries and templates, exporting to SVG, PNG and PDF, and plugging into Google Drive and Docs, SharePoint, OneDrive and Teams, Confluence and Jira, GitHub, VS Code and Notion, with files from 2005 still opening in the current version; the honest limits come from the project's own repository, which states it is a diagramming application and not an SVG editor, that real-time collaborative editing is not currently supported there, that outside pull requests are not accepted, and that its icon and stencil libraries may not be used inside Atlassian products without permission, and the editor expects a recent browser (Chrome 123, Firefox 120 or Safari 17.5 and later).

Vivaldi

Free, donations invitedDesktop, Android, iOSAndroid Police, Oct 3

A browser that ships the parts most people bolt on, with tracker and ad blocking under Settings, Privacy and Security that also hides cookie banners and can be tuned per site, full-page Capture screenshots, Periodic Reload on intervals from one to 90 minutes, mouse gestures, Quick Commands on F2 and Command Chains that fire several actions at once, plus tab stacks, workspaces and tab tiling, built-in mail, calendar, feed reader and notes, end-to-end encrypted sync, and extension support on Android from version 8.2, which Chrome on phones still does not offer; the caveats are that it is a great deal of browser to take on and Android Police's Anu Joy stops short of saying anyone needs to switch, that the bundled VPN is an integration with Proton rather than a Vivaldi service, and that the download is free with donations invited rather than charged.

F-Droid 2.0

Free, donation fundedAndroid, APK from the siteAndroid Police, Oct 3

The free and open source Android app store, rebuilt on September 24, 2026 in what the project calls its largest app update in ten years, with Kotlin Compose and Material styling, navigation reduced to Discover, Search and My Apps, search that finally reads descriptions, categories and translated text rather than exact app names, expanded categories including 17 game genres, and installs and updates that use Android's new pre-approval interface so background update checks are on by default; the caveats are a long list the project publishes itself, since support for Android 6 had to be dropped, the Privileged Extension is not currently supported, the app-wiping feature is gone, the redesigned nearby sharing missed the first release, Tor auto-detection was removed, the rollout runs over several weeks after 14 test releases, and the app still arrives as an APK downloaded from f-droid.org, which the project warns is the very path Google's installation changes put under threat; Android Police's Dhruv Bhutani calls it a massive step forward while saying plainly it is not night-and-day for existing users and may not replace Droidify for him yet.

One SCAN formula replaces a whole column of running totals in a spreadsheet

About 20 minutesExcel in Microsoft 365MakeUseOf front, Oct 3

=SCAN(0, range, LAMBDA) MakeUseOf's Adaeze Uche explains that SCAN walks a range one value at a time and returns every step along the way, so =SCAN(0, A2:A8, LAMBDA(a, v, a + v)) spills a running total down the column from a single cell, =SCAN(0, B2:B9, LAMBDA(a, v, MAX(a, v))) tracks a running best, and =SCAN(0, C2:C9, LAMBDA(a, v, IF(v="Active", a + 1, a))) counts only the rows that qualify, with the accumulator holding what came before and the current value arriving as v; her caveats are that the function needs Excel in Microsoft 365 or Excel 2024 and later, and that the starting value has to suit the operation, which means 1 rather than 0 when the calculation multiplies.
