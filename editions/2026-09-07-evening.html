Turn your router's transmit power down to about half, and a crowded home Wi-Fi network gets steadier

The loudest radio in the house is not the one that gets heard.

MakeUseOf's Oliver Haslam, writing on September 7, makes the case that a router left at maximum transmit power causes the instability it is supposed to prevent. Phones and smart home gadgets carry small antennas and, as he puts it, "can't compete with the brute force of a router shouting over them", so their replies get lost even while the router's own signal arrives strong. He names three more costs of running flat out: high-power signals bounce off hard surfaces inside the house and come back as delayed echoes, neighboring routers are forced to compete and produce Co-Channel Interference that makes networks pause, and devices cling to the strongest signal instead of handing off to a nearer access point on a mesh. The fix is one setting: open the router's management page, usually at 192.168.0.1 or 10.0.0.1, find Advanced Wireless Settings or Transmit Power Control, choose Medium or roughly the 50% mark, then restart the router and give everything a little while to settle back down.

The tool shelf new tonight

AppControl

FreeWindows desktopXDA, Sep 7

AppControl is a free Windows replacement for Task Manager whose real difference is memory: instead of showing only what a process is doing this second, it keeps days of CPU, GPU, and memory activity, with temperature history alongside, so you can scroll back to the moment something started straining the machine. It describes running processes in plain language, lets you disable an app, block a publisher, or write a custom rule, and raises an alert when an app reaches your webcam, microphone, or location, with the option to block or investigate. The current download is version 1.4.0.415 at 20.2 MB. Two caveats are worth carrying in: the makers say they built it as a tool they wanted for themselves and plan to offer optional premium features someday in the future, so free today is not a promise about every future feature, and XDA's Yash Patel, who wrote it up on September 7, is candid that it "didn't magically make my PC faster" and only made the diagnosis clearer, while warning against switching off components you do not understand. The AI tie-in is opt-in and separate: an open-source MCP extension that hands system data to a tool like Claude, Cursor, Gemini, OpenAI, or Windsurf, off by default.

Firefox Focus

FreeAndroid and iPhone browserAndroid Police, Sep 5

Firefox Focus is Mozilla's stripped-down mobile browser, free on Android and iOS, and it treats privacy as the default rather than a mode you have to remember: Mozilla says it "blocks a wide range of common trackers by default including social trackers and those sticky ones that come from things like Facebook ads", and a single erase button on the search field clears your history, passwords, and cookies. The practical side effect Mozilla claims is speed, since pages stripped of trackers "require less data and load much faster". Android Police's Marvin Velasco, writing on September 5, describes a radical simplicity that quietly made it his default browser, with blocking on automatically and the session cleared on exit. The caveats are the design itself, and they are real: no browsing history, no extensions, no syncing between devices, four bookmarks reachable only at startup, and basic tabs with no real multitasking, plus the occasional search engine that answers permanent private mode with a CAPTCHA. Velasco says plainly that this should not be your only browser if you need bookmarks or a session that survives.

Turn your router's transmit power down to about half, and a crowded home Wi-Fi network gets steadier

About 10 minutesYour router's admin page and passwordMakeUseOf front page, Sep 7

One setting, then restart MakeUseOf's Oliver Haslam, writing on September 7, sends you to Transmit Power Control, usually filed under Advanced Wireless Settings once you sign in to the router at 192.168.0.1 or 10.0.0.1, and recommends Medium or about the 50% mark instead of maximum. His reasoning is that a router at full power drowns out the small antennas in phones and smart home devices, bounces echoes off hard surfaces indoors, forces neighboring networks into Co-Channel Interference that makes them pause, and keeps devices locked onto the loudest signal rather than roaming to a nearer access point. The article's images show TP-Link, Netgear Orbi, ASUS, and GL.iNet gear, and he says to restart the router afterward and let things settle. One thing the article does not do is weigh the trade: it names no downside to lowering power, so walk to your weakest room after the change and step back up a notch if coverage there suffers.

Seven Windows shortcuts that replace the mouse trips you make in File Explorer all day

Ten minutes to learnA Windows PCXDA front page, Sep 7

Learn two, then two more XDA's Tanveer Singh, writing on September 7, lists the seven he wishes he had known sooner. Win and E opens a fresh File Explorer window without hunting for the taskbar icon. Backspace moves up one folder, Alt with the left and right arrows retrace and advance along the exact path you took, and Alt with the up arrow jumps to the parent folder. Alt and Enter opens Properties for whatever is selected, which he says changes the game once it replaces the right-click. Holding Shift while clicking a taskbar icon, or clicking it with the scroll wheel, opens a second instance of an app, and holding Ctrl and Shift as you launch one starts it as administrator. Win and comma peeks at the desktop while every window stays exactly where it is, and Win with the up arrow maximizes the active window.

Strip the Windows 11 taskbar and Start menu back to the parts you actually use

About 15 minutesWindows 11MakeUseOf productivity, Sep 6

Four toggles, then four more MakeUseOf's Gregory Gibson, writing on September 6, works through Settings in order. Under Personalization and then Taskbar he switches off Search, Task View, Widgets, and Resume. Under Personalization and then Start he turns off recently added apps, recommended files, tips, and suggestions. Under Privacy and security, then Search permissions, he disables Search highlights, and to stop web suggestions inside the Start menu he adds a DisableSearchBoxSuggestions value from Windows Terminal, which is the one step that edits the registry and deserves a careful hand. He reports less visual clutter and better responsiveness on a slow connection, and keeps what he removed on the keyboard instead: Win and S for search, Win and Tab for Task View.

Open a second window on the same Excel workbook and let Alt and Tab do the sheet switching

Under two minutesMicrosoft ExcelHow-To Geek front page, Sep 7

View, then New Window How-To Geek's Tony Phillips, writing on September 7, stopped fighting his habit of reaching for Alt and Tab and made Excel fit it instead. Click View and then New Window, and Excel opens a second window onto the same workbook rather than a copy: the title bar labels them with the file name followed by a 1 and a 2, and an edit made in either window saves to the one file. That turns Alt and Tab into a worksheet switcher, which he finds far less tedious than pressing Ctrl with Page Up or Page Down when the two sheets sit several tabs apart. He points to Excel's Watch Window as the alternative for the narrower job of keeping an eye on a few selected cells without moving between sheets at all.
