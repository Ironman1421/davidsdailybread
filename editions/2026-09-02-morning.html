Dell posts a record $47 billion quarter as its AI server backlog reaches $95 billion

Record AI server orders left a backlog worth roughly two full quarters of company revenue, and Dell raised its outlook anyway.

Dell Technologies reported second-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $47.0 billion, up 58% year over year, with non-GAAP diluted earnings of $7.04 per share, up 203%. AI server orders reached $60.9 billion in the quarter and AI server revenue doubled to $16.4 billion, leaving a record ending backlog of $95.0 billion. Its Infrastructure Solutions Group brought in $31.8 billion, up 89%, while the client business rose 20% to $15.0 billion. Dell now guides full-year revenue to about $192.0 billion at the midpoint and expects roughly $74 billion of AI server revenue for the year.

Letters to the King
When you counted your fighting men and it grieved you afterward, what had you actually wanted?
From the Baker's own shelf

Friend, you have found the bruise and pressed it, and I will not pretend you missed. What did I want? A number I could hold. I had come a long way from a field of sheep, and somewhere on that road I began wanting proof that the road had been worth walking. Once I faced a giant with a sling and five smooth stones, and the LORD was the whole of my confidence. Later I had a kingdom, and a kingdom can be counted. A tally is a warmer thing to sleep beside than a memory of mercy, so I counted. I wanted to say: this much is mine, this much is safe, this is how large I have become. The grief came after because the number answered nothing I had actually asked. It told me my strength and said not one word about my God. A man may build a strong fence around a field he does not own and feel very secure inside it. So if you are counting something tonight, and I suspect you are, ask what the counting is for. Counting is not the sin. Counting instead of trusting is. Keep your books, tally your savings, weigh your work; only ask also for a heart that numbers its days. And when you find you have counted the wrong thing, do what I did far too slowly: say so plainly, and turn back to the One who was never in the arithmetic to begin with.

– David, son of Jesse
