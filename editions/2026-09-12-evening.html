Raycast 2.0 puts a launcher, clipboard history, snippets, and window management behind one keystroke

One shortcut absorbs the app switching, clipboard hunting, and window dragging you repeat a hundred times a day.

Raycast calls itself a collection of productivity tools inside an extendable launcher, and the 2.0 release rebuilt that foundation around AI that can act across apps, built-in automation for recurring tasks, and a Projects view for ongoing work; you can connect your own ChatGPT or Claude subscription rather than buy another one. The free plan carries the parts most people reach for daily: clipboard history, quicklinks, snippets, a calculator, window management, and access to thousands of extensions. Windows is no longer a promise: the app is available now for Windows 10 (21H2 or newer) and Windows 11 from the Microsoft Store, alongside the Mac and iOS versions. Pro runs $10 a month or $96 a year and adds Raycast AI credits, unlimited clipboard history, cloud sync, dictation, and a translator.

A keyboard launcher that folds clipboard history, snippets, quicklinks, a calculator, and window management behind one shortcut with thousands of extensions on top, though the free plan keeps only three months of clipboard history and leaves Raycast AI to the paid Pro tier.

Adobe Express

Free; Premium $9.99/moDesktop browsers and phonesMakeUseOf, Sep 12

Adobe's free plan costs nothing and needs no credit card, running in a desktop browser or on a phone with 5GB of storage and 10 days of version history, and MakeUseOf reports using it for highlights, shadows, saturation, warmth, sharpness, cropping, and background removal, while warning it is not the place for detailed masking, color grading, lens corrections, or RAW files.

LocalSend

Free and open sourceWindows, Mac, Linux, mobileXDA Developers, Sep 12

An open source app that sends photos, videos, and documents straight to a nearby device over encrypted HTTPS with no account, no login, and no cloud upload, which XDA offers as the cure for emailing files to yourself, though every device has to sit on the same Wi-Fi network and it is built for immediate transfers rather than ongoing sync or remote recipients.

Relic

Free tier; Pro $7/moWindows, Mac, Linux, mobileProduct Hunt front page, Sep 12

A clipboard manager that keeps everything you copy and syncs it under end to end encryption with a key the company says only you hold, capturing automatically on desktop and by share sheet on a phone, but the free tier holds only your last 500 copies plus 25 pinned items across three devices and 250 MB.

Every shelf item is available today; no waitlists, no vaporware.

Build an Obsidian knowledge base out of old exports, voice memos, and PDFs instead of typing new notes

About 45 minutesObsidian and a vaultHow-To Geek front page, Sep 12

Import, record, drop in The Importer plugin pulls in Google Keep notes exported through Google Takeout along with Markdown from apps like Joplin or JotterPad, the Audio Recorder core plugin under Settings then Core Plugins captures spoken notes while you read or after a meeting, and PDFs join the vault by dragging them in on desktop or tapping the paperclip on mobile.

Speak into Google Keep and let Keep Live turn a rambling list into a structured note

About 15 minutesGoogle Keep with Keep LiveAndroid Police, Sep 12

Say it, then tap Done Keep Live listens while you describe what you need, such as the documents for a passport renewal, structures the result into a note, and accepts follow-ups like splitting an itinerary into two notes or adding to an existing one, though a split leaves the original behind and you cannot ask it to set a reminder.

Give MacroDroid three standing rules and stop toggling Wi-Fi, opening maps, and silencing your phone by hand

About 30 minutesAn Android phoneAndroid Police, Sep 12

Trigger, action, constraint A geofence switches Wi-Fi and mobile data as you arrive home and leave it, an Android Auto connection opens YouTube Music and navigation toward work, and a calendar event mutes the phone and restores the sound when the meeting ends.

Three Excel functions that retire manual filtering: XLOOKUP, SUMIFS with COUNTIFS, and FILTER

About 30 minutesExcel with dynamic arraysMakeUseOf front page, Sep 12

Stop counting columns XLOOKUP asks only what to find, where to look, and what to return, SUMIFS and COUNTIFS total or count rows against as many as 127 criteria pairs while leaving the source data untouched, and FILTER returns every matching row from one formula and updates itself when the data changes.
