In a first trial, CAR T-cell therapy left three of six severe rheumatoid arthritis patients medication free

A cancer technique, aimed at the immune system instead, held up in its first clinical test.

Six patients with severe rheumatoid arthritis, three women and three men aged 31 to 69, were treated at the Charité hospital in Berlin with CD19 CAR T cells, the same class of engineered immune cells used against blood cancers. Disease activity fell markedly in all six, and three were in sustained remission without any rheumatoid arthritis medication during follow-up of up to one year, while the autoantibodies typical of the disease declined sharply. Cytokine release syndrome was mild to moderate in the participants, and the team reported no severe neurological complications or other serious adverse events. The results were published in Nature Medicine, and a second phase will compare the therapy against an approved B-cell drug in ten more patients.

What does true repentance look like on an ordinary Tuesday, when there is no prophet in the room?
From the Baker's own shelf

Friend, I know that Tuesday. No prophet at the door, no harp, no fire falling; only the ordinary hours and a heart that knows exactly what it did. Repentance there is quiet work. It is the turning itself, not the noise around it: you name the thing plainly, without softening it into a smaller word. You go to the one you wronged before you go to bed. You put back what you took, and where you cannot put it back, you say so out loud. Then you walk the other way, and you keep walking on Wednesday, which is the harder half. I had Nathan once, and I needed him; but the mercy I found that day was never his to give. It waited for me on unremarkable mornings too, and it is waiting for you. A broken and contrite heart He will not despise, and no prophet is required to hand it over.

– David, son of Jesse
