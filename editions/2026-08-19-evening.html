Make a free Claude account last: pick the lighter model, lower the effort setting, and hand off before the limit

The free tier runs on a rolling five-hour window, and most of what drains it is habit rather than the work itself.

How-To Geek's Vishwamoorthy Ramakrishnan writes as a free-tier user who kept hitting Claude's rolling five-hour session limits, and every fix he lands on changes how a prompt is built rather than what it asks for. He states the persona, the task, the output format, and the context up front, asks for a step-by-step plan before any execution, and hands the mechanical steps off to other free chatbots. He also matches the model to the job: Haiku carries quick answers, summaries, and simple extraction at the lightest rate use, Sonnet is held back for coding, writing, analysis, and multi-step work, and the effort control, which defaults to Medium, comes down when a task does not need it. Before a session runs out he has Claude write a handoff summary he can upload into the next chat, and he keeps standing instructions and reference files in Projects, which Anthropic's documentation caps at five for free accounts.

The tool shelf new tonight

Grist

Free tier; Pro $10/user/moWeb app; self-host with DockerMakeUseOf, Aug 19

Grist is a relational spreadsheet: columns are named and typed, records link across tables without a VLOOKUP, and charts, calendar views, and summary tables come off that same data, with forms, Python formulas, and real-time collaboration included on the free plan; the honest limit is that free hosting stops at 5,000 records per document and 10 members, so a tracker that keeps growing means either $10 per user per month or running the free open-source Community Edition on your own machine.

LocalSend

Free, Apache 2.0, no adsDesktop and phone, local Wi-FiMakeUseOf, Aug 19

Install it on the phone and the computer, pick the files, tap the other device, and the transfer runs over your own Wi-Fi with end-to-end encryption and an optional PIN, and the project states plainly that it works completely offline, that your data never leaves the local network, and that there are no ads, tracking, or hidden costs; the catch follows from that design, since both devices have to be on the same network at the same time, so it does nothing for a file you need to send across town.

NextDNS

Free tier; Pro $1.99/moSet on the router or per deviceMakeUseOf, Aug 19

Point a router or one device at NextDNS instead of the resolver your provider hands you and the logs become a live feed of every domain each device in the house tried to reach, with the device name and the timestamp, a block control beside them, and settings for where the logs live and how long they are kept, from an hour out to two years; MakeUseOf's Pankil Shah names the tradeoff without softening it, that seeing all of that traffic means trusting NextDNS with it, and the free plan covers 300,000 queries a month before it goes back to answering like ordinary non-blocking DNS.

Flow Launcher

Free, open sourceWindows 10 and laterMakeUseOf, Aug 19

One hotkey, Alt+Space by default, opens a single box that searches apps, files, browser bookmarks, and Windows settings, runs a calculation, fires a shell or system command, or sends the query to the web, with a plugin store to add more; the caveat is that it is Windows 10 and later only, and its file search leans on Everything or Windows Search being in place underneath it.

Every shelf item is available today; no waitlists, no vaporware.

The workflows follow along at home

Let Windows empty its own temp files, Recycle Bin, and stale downloads on a schedule you choose

10 minutes to set upA Windows PCMakeUseOf front page, Aug 19

Storage Sense, switched on Settings, then System, then Storage turns on automatic user content cleanup, which clears the Recycle Bin after 30 days by default and can put the Downloads folder, which otherwise never deletes anything, on a 1, 14, 30, or 60 day timer.

Trade a slice of your top speed for video calls that stop freezing for everyone in the house

About 30 minutesA router with a QoS settingXDA front page, Aug 19

Quality of Service, deliberately XDA's Ty Sherback turned on QoS in a TP-Link Archer router, entered the real upload and download figures from the ISP, set priority percentages, and flagged the devices that matter, accepting a lower ceiling because QoS drops the router into software forwarding that consumer silicon cannot push gigabit traffic through.

Turn on the taskbar right-click that force-quits a frozen app without opening Task Manager

2 minutes, onceWindows 11How-To Geek front page, Aug 19

One toggle, then every taskbar Windows 11 hides an End Task switch under Settings, System, Advanced (For developers on some builds), and How-To Geek's Tony Phillips is clear about the cost: it kills the process outright rather than asking it to close, it takes every window of that app at once, and unsaved work goes with it.
