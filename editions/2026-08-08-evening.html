Stop uploading PDFs to ChatGPT: six file types that keep your tables, slides, and documents intact

The PDF is the easiest thing to hand an AI chatbot, and it is usually the worst thing you can hand it.

Oluwademilade Afolabi argues that exporting to PDF flattens structure, so ChatGPT ends up reading a flattened, static version of your spreadsheet instead of the spreadsheet. He names six formats that keep the structure intact: CSV for a single clean table, XLSX for a workbook whose sheet boundaries and calculated values survive, JSON for nested app exports and logs, DOCX for prose you want reorganized by heading, PPTX for slide order and information density, and PNG for a screenshot of an error message, an interface, or a receipt. He is honest about the limits: what you can upload depends on your model, plan, and workspace settings, embedded images inside DOCX and PPTX are not officially supported, image reading gets unreliable with tiny text, rotated pages, or complicated charts, and he advises validating a JSON file before you send it and keeping the dataset open beside you when exact values matter.

Craft

A block-based editor for notes, tasks, and documents that lets you drag whole sections into a new order, nest subpages under a project, and keep daily notes, tasks, and a calendar in one place, and the free plan draws its lines clearly: 1,500 blocks, 1 GB of storage, 25 MB per upload, seven days of version history, and 15 AI credits, while XDA's Parth Shah grants it is not a complete replacement for Word on complex formatting, academic references, or heavily structured corporate documents.

Replace six separate apps with one browser's built-in mail, calendar, notes, and screenshot tools

An evening to set upVivaldi on desktopMakeUseOf front page, Aug 8

One window, six jobs Shaheer Khan moved his browsing, note-taking, screenshots, email, calendar, and YouTube into Vivaldi, stacking and tiling tabs to keep related pages together, separating contexts with workspaces, writing in the markdown notepad, and running the built-in mail and calendar clients, and he is candid about the cost of it: Vivaldi will give a machine with less memory a run for its money, and the browser felt overwhelming to him at first even though it can import your Chrome or Edge data.

Pick one clear Wi-Fi channel by hand instead of letting the router choose it for you

About 20 minutesRouter admin accessMakeUseOf Trending Now

Analyzer first, then lock Oluwaseun Bamisile checked the networks around him with a Wi-Fi analyzer app, then replaced his router's automatic channel selection with a fixed one, settling on channel 6 because 1, 6, and 11 are the only non-overlapping channels on 2.4GHz, and naming 36, 40, 44, and 48 as the best starting points on 5GHz, and he says his connection became more stable without claiming a number for it, adding that the picks are not set in stone: the right channel depends on your region and the congestion nearby, and which channels a router may legally use depends on your country, so its region setting has to be correct.

Let a Samsung phone quiet itself, stretch its battery, and call out the time you lose to YouTube

About 30 minutesA Samsung Galaxy phoneHow-To Geek front page

Settings, Modes and Routines Zunaid Ali builds routines that switch on Do Not Disturb, power saving, and grayscale between midnight and 5 AM, run a thirty-minute nap timer that reads a message aloud, and tighten battery saving in two steps, first at 50 percent and again at 30 percent with data saver and dimmed brightness, then adds a Gmail filter on the desktop site that keeps chosen senders out of Spam and in Primary, and one loop in the free Automate app that tells him how long he has actually been sitting on YouTube; he estimates the set saves him about thirty minutes a day, and he grants that the schedules need adjusting every few months and that a nap timer cannot ring a real alarm sound.
