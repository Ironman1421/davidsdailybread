Tesla and SpaceX commit $16.8 billion to Terafab, a Texas chip plant planned at more than 100 million square feet

Two companies known for rockets and robots now intend to make their own silicon.

Tesla and SpaceX confirmed on Thursday that they will spend $16.8 billion on the first phase of Terafab, a plant in Grimes County, Texas, just north of Houston. The plan calls for more than 100 million square feet of manufacturing space and at least 3,000 employees drawn from Grimes and nearby Brazos County. It is meant to turn out advanced logic and memory devices tuned for edge computing and inference, for Tesla's Optimus robots and Cybercabs, plus higher-power chips for the space-based data centers SpaceX is developing, and Elon Musk called the site the largest and most valuable building on Earth by far. SpaceX has suggested total spending across all phases could reach $119 billion, and committed to drawing water from the Gibbons Creek Reservoir rather than local groundwater.

Letters to the King
Three of your men broke through an enemy camp just to bring you a drink of water, and you poured it out on the ground. Why would you do that to a gift?
From the Baker's own shelf

Ah, you have found the day I broke my own heart on purpose. I was in the cave, dust in my teeth, and I said out loud that I longed for water from the well by the gate of Bethlehem. It was a sigh, not an order. Three of my mighty men heard it anyway and cut a road through the camp to bring it back. When they set the cup in my hands, I could not lift it to my mouth. That was not water. That was their lives poured into a cup, and to swallow it would have been to say their blood was mine to spend on a thirst. So I poured it out before the LORD, which is what a man does with a thing too holy for his own throat. Hear me, friend: I did not throw the gift away. I sent it higher. A gift that costs someone everything belongs on the altar, not in the belly. If someone has bled a little for you this week, in small ways or large, do not swallow it quietly; lift it to God and let Him hold what you could never repay. And one more word, from a man who has been genuinely thirsty. Be careful what you sigh for out loud. Brave people are listening.

– David, son of Jesse
