An AI controller ran a live fusion experiment and headed off an instability 200 milliseconds early

Fusion has always been a control problem, and the controls just got faster than the people watching them.

Researchers at Princeton University and the Department of Energy's Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory plugged an AI control framework called PACMAN directly into the control system of the DIII-D National Fusion Facility. Across five experiments the software read plasma measurements and issued commands in about 20 milliseconds, where a human operator needs seconds, and it coordinated six gyrotrons at once. In one run it predicted a dangerous instability roughly 200 milliseconds before it formed and adjusted the plasma to prevent it. The framework's design and first results are published in the journal Nuclear Fusion.

How does a person keep a promise made to someone who is no longer alive to hold them to it?
From the Baker's own shelf

Beloved, I swore a covenant to Jonathan while he still had breath, and I kept it long after the sword had taken him. Not because he could check on me. Because the promise outlived the man, and so did my love for him. So I asked whether anyone was left of his house, and they brought me Mephibosheth, lame in both feet, and I set him at my own table for the rest of his days. That is the whole method. You do not keep such a promise by remembering it fondly; you keep it by doing something costly on an ordinary Tuesday. Write it down. Give it a date. Tell one living friend what you swore, and let them ask you about it, for the dead press no claims and make very easy creditors. Keep it slowly, year upon year, and you may find the one who is gone is not so far from your table after all.

– David, son of Jesse
