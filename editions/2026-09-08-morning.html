Stoke Space raises $1 billion more to chase a fully reusable rocket

A hard engineering bet that nobody has yet pulled off just drew the kind of money that buys years.

Stoke Space has closed an initial $1 billion of a Series E round led by Point72 Ventures and Spark Capital, bringing its total raised to $2.3 billion. The company is building a rocket in which both the booster and the payload-carrying second stage return to Earth for reuse, a capability no operator has achieved, and it protects that second stage on reentry by running super-cooled liquid hydrogen through the thermal shielding. Its first vehicle, Nova Pathfinder, is expected to fly in early 2027 carrying three metric tons to low-Earth orbit, with a second-generation Nova targeted for 2029 at fifteen metric tons. Chief executive Andy Lapsa said the round is "really to scale," funding production, flight frequency, and the next vehicle.

Letters to the King
What is the difference between godly grief over a wrong and simply hating yourself for it?
From the Baker's own shelf

Beloved, I have known both, and they do not smell alike. When Nathan came to me with his little story of the poor man's lamb, my first word was not about myself; it was, I have sinned against the LORD. That is the mark of godly sorrow: it turns toward God, it names the wrong plainly, it makes what amends it can, and then it lets itself be forgiven. Self-hatred, meanwhile, walks in circles. It talks a great deal about you and very little about the one you harmed, and it never quite gets around to restitution, because loathing feels like payment and is not. So put two questions to your own heart tonight. Has my sorrow moved me toward the person I wronged, or only deeper into myself? And when mercy is held out, do I take it? When the worst had come and gone, I rose from the floor, washed, worshipped, and ate bread, for grief that has been carried to God knows how to stop. The other kind never learns where to put its coat down.

– David, son of Jesse
