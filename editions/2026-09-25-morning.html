Akamai signs an $11.6 billion, seven-year computing agreement with Anthropic

The contracts behind artificial intelligence are now longer than most corporate strategies, and larger than most balance sheets.

Akamai Technologies announced an $11.6 billion contractual commitment from Anthropic over seven years, aimed at the AI company's accelerating CPU workload demands on Akamai Cloud's distributed infrastructure. Alongside the contract, Akamai issued Anthropic a warrant for non-voting convertible Series B preferred stock representing 7.7 million shares on an as-converted basis, or up to roughly 5 percent of Akamai's outstanding common stock, at an exercise price of $111.33 per share; about 2 percent of that vests with the announced commitment. The two companies may extend the relationship by up to $9 billion more over the same term, which Akamai describes as a total potential commitment of approximately $20 billion. Chief executive and co-founder Tom Leighton said Anthropic is advancing the AI revolution and that the company chose Akamai's capabilities for building and operating AI infrastructure at scale.

How do you tell the difference between godly ambition and plain wanting?
From the Baker's own shelf

I have known both, and I knew them in the same lifetime. When I carried bread to my brothers in the valley and saw the giant, something rose in me that wanted the name of God honored. When I walked my roof one evening and saw what was not mine, something rose in me that only wanted. The two feel alike at the start, friend. They part company at the question of who gets smaller. Godly ambition makes room: it can be shared, it can wait, it can be handed to another man's son and still be good. Plain wanting cannot bear to be shared, cannot bear to wait, and always needs one more thing before it will rest. So test your desire the slow way. Tell it to someone who loves you, and watch whether you flinch in the telling. Ask whether it would still be worth doing if your name were left off the stone. Then notice what it does to the people nearest you, for holy ambition tends to feed them and plain wanting tends to bill them. I wanted to build the temple. The answer was no. I gathered the cedar and the iron anyway and left them for Solomon, and that was the first honest ambition I ever had, because it was the first one I would not live to see finished.

– David, son of Jesse
