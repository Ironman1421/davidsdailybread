# X reply-led growth cadence

Status: proposed operating cadence; no reply or external action authorized by
this document

Decision owner: David Friedhof

## Starting experiment

Run a four-week manual reply experiment on five staffed days per week. Use two
approval windows:

- Morning: 7:00 to 8:00 a.m. Pacific.
- Late afternoon: 4:00 to 5:00 p.m. Pacific.

Each window may produce zero, one, or two approved replies. Four replies per
day is a hard ceiling, not a quota. Never lower the BREAD or opportunity-score
threshold to fill a window. The existing limit of no more than two proactive
replies in a rolling hour still applies.

The experiment can therefore produce at most 80 replies in four weeks. It may
produce far fewer. Quality, visibility, and qualified profile conversion decide
whether the channel works.

## Weekly rhythm

### Monday through Thursday

- Scout current conversations in the two staffed windows.
- Prefer conversations where DDB can add a sourced fact, plain-language
  translation, or practical next step.
- Present only candidates that clear all existing identity, context, BREAD,
  opportunity, frequency, and source checks.
- David approves the exact parent URL and exact reply text.
- An authorized human posts through the official X interface.

### Friday

- Continue only with unusually strong, current opportunities.
- Capture the week's outcome snapshots and review hidden or probable-spam
  inspections.
- Record which target tiers, beats, and reply shapes produced qualified profile
  visits, direct follows, or useful continuing conversation.

### Weekend

- No standing proactive-reply quota.
- Respond manually to genuine continuing conversations when appropriate and
  within the daily cap.
- Prepare the next week's watchlist without following, liking, posting, or
  messaging automatically.

## First comparison

Compare two reply shapes first:

1. `practical_application`
2. `plain_language_translation`

Hold target tier, opportunity-score band, discovery window, and topic quality as
stable as practical. Do not declare a winner until both shapes have at least
eight measured observations. Other shapes remain available when they are the
only honest contribution, but they are not part of the first controlled
comparison.

## Target allocation

The watchlist should favor primary sources, maintainers, identifiable experts,
credible reporters, and durable institutions. Raw follower count is secondary.
Across a normal week, seek a balanced opportunity set:

- AI tools and workflows that support the evening Field Guide.
- Technology, markets, and science conversations that fit the morning edition.
- Faith conversations only when DDB can contribute naturally and directly.

Never insert faith language into an unrelated technology conversation, or use a
large account merely because it offers attention.

## Profile conversion prerequisites

Do not start the experiment until the existing readiness gate is complete:

- The profile states a clear reason to follow.
- The faceless mark and banner are recognizable.
- The canonical website link works.
- A sourced briefing is pinned.
- Recent morning and evening posts demonstrate the promise.
- The correct account, MFA, recovery, operator, Premium tier, and analytics
  availability are assessed.

Replies create discovery. The profile and recent work must convert that
discovery into a return visit or follow.

## Additional growth actions

These actions complement replies without turning DDB into an engagement bot:

- Keep the canonical morning and evening broadcasts consistent.
- Manually publish at most one distinct edition-derived source card, useful
  fact, or question when it adds value and receives review.
- Maintain private X Lists by beat for higher-quality scouting.
- Continue a conversation when another person responds, while respecting the
  overall daily cap and context checks.
- Use the evening tools and workflows library as the destination for practical
  discovery, not as a link pasted into unsolicited replies.
- Review profile conversion and returning-site evidence weekly, not only likes
  and impressions.

Do not automate likes, follows, quote posts, trend participation, unsolicited
mentions, or replies. Do not buy followers, trade engagement, or post generic
praise at scale.

## Decision points

- After 16 measured replies: check visibility, probable-spam placement,
  operational burden, and whether both initial shapes have usable samples.
- After 30 days: decide whether reply-led discovery deserves another month.
- After at least 50 complete reply snapshots: consider whether volume may be
  tested. Six replies in a day still requires three staffed windows and a new
  explicit approval.
- After 100 measured replies: decide whether the channel produces enough
  qualified profile conversion to justify ongoing scouting capacity.

Stop sooner for an account warning, policy signal, correction, credential
concern, hidden reply, probable-spam placement, or repeated low-quality traffic.
